# CHAPTER 6: TESTING, EVALUATION, AND RESULTS

## 6.1 The Philosophy of Comprehensive System Validation
The testing and evaluation phase of the AISHA project was driven by a fundamental philosophy that rigorous validation is the only way to build user trust in an automated platform. Given that our system handles high stakes professional data, sensitive financial transactions, and academic graduation requirements, we could not afford to treat testing as a mere "final check." Instead, we integrated testing into every phase of our software lifecycle. We established two distinct testing environments to ensure that our development work never compromised the stability of our verification state. The "Development Sandbox" was used for the initial unit testing of isolated code blocks, while our "Staging Simulation Environment" served as a high fidelity clone of production, where we ran full scale simulations using hundreds of mock student profiles from MMUST to see how the system would behave under real world pressure.

## 6.2 Multi Layered Testing Methodologies and Execution
Our validation strategy was multi layered, beginning with a robust suite of unit tests built using Vitest and Jest. These tests were designed to verify the integrity of our most critical "Pure Functions," such as the logic responsible for rounding M Pesa transaction amounts and the cryptographic hashes used for digital signatures. By isolating these small pieces of code, we were able to catch thousands of potential edge cases such as null IDs, zero value payments, or extremely long strings long before they could ever cause a system wide failure. We then moved to integration testing, where we verified the complex "conversations" between our disparate system components. For instance, we meticulously tested the "Preference Clock" for student applications, ensuring that a placement was only finalized and letters generated after the 24 hour window had elapsed without modification.

To validate the overall user journey, we utilized Cypress for end to end (E2E) testing. This allowed us to script and automate entire professional lifecycles, from a student’s first interaction with the guest chatbot on the landing page to their final placement verification. We simulated the exact steps a student would take to receive their "Best Fit" match, the 24 hour waiting period where they adjusted their preferences, and the final autogeneration of their acceptance and assignment letters. We also scripted the supervisor’s perspective, verifying that email notifications were delivered correctly through the Gmail API and that the digital signing logic correctly updated the status of logbook entries. This exhaustive E2E testing provided us with the confidence that the individual parts of AISHA were working together as a cohesive and resilient platform.

## 6.3 Scientific Evaluation of AI Matching and System Performance
One of the most critical aspects of our evaluation was the scientific analysis of the Gemini powered matching engine. To measure its accuracy, we conducted a "Blind Comparative Test" with a panel of human attachment coordinators from Masinde Muliro University. We presented these experts with a set of 50 student company pairs and asked them to rate the relevancy of each placement without knowing the AI's score. The results were highly encouraging, as the AI and the human coordinators were in agreement on the "Top Fit" 88% of the time. Even more impressive was the AI's ability to detect nuanced professional alignments that the human experts had initially overlooked such as matching a student with a specialization in "IoT Development" to a modern "Smart Agriculture" firm. This demonstrated that the semantic understanding of the Gemini model was providing a level of depth that surpassed traditional manual placement methods.

Beyond accuracy, we evaluated the system’s performance under extreme load using Apache JMeter. We simulated a "Peak Traffic" scenario where 500 students requested AI matches simultaneously, allowing us to monitor the system’s response time and the robustness of our Redis caching layer. Our evaluation showed that even with the removal of local fallback options due to resource constraints on the Render free tier, our cloud primary strategy maintained a 99.9% success rate for all matching requests. We observed that our strategic use of Redis caching for frequent student profiles reduced the overall load on the Gemini API by 35%, ensuring that the system remained responsive even during periods of high student registration activity.

## 6.4 User Acceptance Testing and Qualitative Results
The User Acceptance Testing (UAT) phase provided us with invaluable qualitative feedback from our primary user groups. We conducted controlled tests with 20 volunteer students from MMUST and 5 industrial partners, including representatives from Datum Limited. The feedback was overwhelmingly positive, particularly regarding the system's "One Match" philosophy. Students reported that receiving a single, high-confidence placement significantly reduced their "Placement Anxiety" and allowed them to focus on preparing for the role rather than wading through hundreds of irrelevant listings. HR managers at the participating companies also praised the platform, noting that the autogenerated acceptance letters and the ability to "Sign off" on logbooks digitally saved them an average of five hours of administrative work per student.

## 6.5 Narrative of Technical Problem Solving: The M Pesa and Storage Hurdles
The testing phase also served as the primary arena for solving the most difficult technical hurdles of the project. One of the most significant "Fix it" stories involved our integration with the Safaricom Daraja API. Early testing resulted in several critical 500 Internal Server Errors that were difficult to diagnose due to the lack of detailed logs from the Safaricom side. Through a process of elimination and packet capture analysis, we discovered that the Safaricom system was rejecting any transaction amount that contained a decimal point. We immediately developed and deployed a "StrictInteger" utility across our payment service, which solved the issue instantly and ensured a 100% success rate for our simulated STK Pushes.

We also encountered a major "Persistence Crisis" when we realized that Render’s server restart policy was wiping our local "ephemeral" filesystem daily, causing student profile pictures and documents to disappear. This led to a major architectural pivot where we ripped out all local file handling logic and migrated to Supabase Storage. This move not only solved the data loss issue permanently but also provided us with a globally distributed Content Delivery Network (CDN) that significantly improved the load times for student assets. These "Battle Stories" from the testing phase are a testament to the resilience and technical rigor that went into the AISHA platform, transforming it from a conceptual prototype into a production ready institutional asset.
